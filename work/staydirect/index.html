01

Context & my role

StayDirect is a web platform for short-term rental hosts who want guests to book them directly instead of through Airbnb. The founder runs 80+ rental properties himself, so the product grew out of his own operations.

Host side · desktop

Import listings from Airbnb or Guesty, get an AI-written guest guidebook for each property, build a direct booking page, send email campaigns to past guests.

Guest side · mobile web

Online check-in before arrival, then a Guest Hub during the stay: door code, Wi-Fi, local tips, check-out steps.

I worked as an outsourced UX/UI designer and was the only designer for the whole project. I designed the product screens and built the design system. The client did not share user research; I worked from design tasks and the founder’s product logic.

Because I can’t show the shipped screens, I rebuilt five key flows from scratch for this case. I used it to look at my own work with fresh eyes: I wrote down the user picture as hypotheses, audited what shipped and rebuilt the design system.

02

Problem

Airbnb owns the relationship with the guest. Hosts pay platform fees on every stay and can’t reach a guest again after check-out. StayDirect’s idea is a loop: collect guest emails through a helpful guidebook, then bring guests back to book directly.

  1. Importlistings
  2. Guidebookfor every stay
  3. Guest emailscollected
  4. Campaignto past guests
  5. Direct bookingno Airbnb fee

The loop only works if both sides find the product easy:

  • Hosts, especially managers with dozens of units, need setup that scales. The shipped onboarding asked to configure listings one by one and only supported Airbnb.
  • Guests need check-in to feel quick and safe. The shipped flow had 6 steps, a marketing survey in the middle and prices that changed between steps.

The visual language had also drifted over four years: three different navigation menus, 5+ accent colours with no clear role and four icon styles. That made the product feel less trustworthy, which matters for a service that asks guests to pay outside Airbnb.

04

Design system: v1 to “Noir Ember”

I built v1 during the project. Looking back, it had the right structure but loose rules: every semantic colour could be used anywhere, spacing was mostly hard-coded, and placeholder text failed contrast (2.5:1). For this case I rebuilt it as v2 and grew it together with the re-created screens.

v1v2 “Noir Ember”
Colour5+ accents, no clear rolesOne brand colour for actions (ember #FF6B2C), blue for highlights, neutrals, status colours
TokensNo scopes, no code namesPrimitives → semantic tokens, scoped, CSS names for developers
ContrastTertiary text 2.5:1, invisible disabled iconsText 4.8:1+, dark text on ember buttons (6.8:1)
TypeTwo familiesGeist only: light for numbers and headlines, medium for UI
ComponentsBuilt page by page50+ components with states, properties and generated docs
Icons4 mixed stylesOne outline set (Lucide), colour from tokens

Every new element from the screens went back into the library, documented, before the screens were rebuilt on it. The system has its own case study.

05

Key decisions

1 Host onboarding: 7 screens to 4 steps that scale

  • Two sources, one choice. Airbnb or Guesty as large option cards; the one for big portfolios carries a “Best for 20+ listings” badge.
  • Instant feedback. Pasting a profile link shows the found host and “24 listings” before the user presses anything.
  • Everything selected by default. An 86-unit import is “deselect what you don’t want”, with search, filters and a disabled row for already-imported units.
  • Set once for all. Support email, Wi-Fi (pulled from Guesty) and check-in times apply to every listing. The shipped flow asked for them per listing.
  • No dead ends. The import runs while the host fills in settings; the last screen gives three clear next steps.

2 Guest check-in: 6 steps to 4

  • Removed the survey step and prefilled details from the Airbnb booking.
  • Clear money. Damage waiver ($49, one-time) or deposit ($500 hold, not charged) as two cards; the total updates live and is repeated next to the button.
  • Fixed progress. Four segments that match four steps. The shipped flow showed 100% on step 5 of 6.
  • A reason to finish. A locked door code on the welcome screen; it unlocks on the last screen together with Wi-Fi and directions.
  • Trust at the right moment. “Your host only sees Verified” next to the ID step.

3 Guest Hub: a home screen that follows the stay

  • Door code and Wi-Fi first, the two things guests search for most.
  • The screen changes with the trip: during the stay, tips and guidebook; on check-out day, a checklist and the rebooking offer.
  • The business moment. “Come back direct and save 10%” appears on check-out day, with a code to copy.

4 Booking page builder

  • Edit and preview side by side; the selected section is outlined in the live preview.
  • AI as a helper, not the only way. Suggestion chips fill the blank prompt; manual settings stay one click away.
  • A real reason to book direct. The “save vs Airbnb” badge is calculated from the host’s own prices.

5 Email marketing dashboard

  • Money first. Direct revenue from email is the hero number; bookings and revenue sit in every campaign row.
  • Data-based suggestions (“312 guests stayed last winter”) instead of an empty “create campaign”.
  • Safe actions. Delete moved from a red button in each row into a “…” menu.

07

Outcome & learnings

I don’t have product metrics I can share, and the re-created flows were not tested in this form, so I don’t claim numbers. What the work produced:

  • A design system the product can grow on: 50+ documented components, scoped tokens with CSS names, accessible contrast by default.
  • Five flows built around the real customer, a host with dozens of units, instead of a single-listing host.
  • A clear product loop on one board: guidebook → guest emails → campaign → direct booking.

No research is not a reason to skip it

Proto-personas, CJMs and an audit made every decision traceable, even as hypotheses.

A system grows best next to real screens

Each flow added components; each component made the next flow faster.

Trust is a UI problem too

Clear money, one visual language and honest copy matter most where a guest pays outside Airbnb.

Being the only designer

Four years alone on a product taught me to document decisions, because nobody else would remember why a screen looks the way it does.

What I would test next: the check-in flow with 5 guests (time to finish, drop-off per step) and the import step with 3 property managers.
